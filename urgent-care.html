Oncology Emergencies
Photo placeholder — nurse comforting a patient (images/onc-emergency.jpg)

Urgent care for cancer patients at any stage of treatment

  • Fever or suspected infection (especially during chemotherapy)
  • Severe pain or sudden worsening of cancer symptoms
  • Dehydration, persistent vomiting or diarrhea
  • Breathing problems or shortness of breath
  • Bleeding
  • Severe anemia or other blood-related problems
  • Treatment reactions and chemotherapy complications
  • Neurologic emergencies (e.g. suspected spinal cord compression)
  • Urinary obstruction
  • Acute complications from advanced cancer
  • Any other urgent oncologic concern
General Medical & Accident Care
Photo placeholder — doctor examining a child patient (images/general-emergency.jpg)

Urgent care for the whole community

  • Accident and injury patients (falls, road traffic accidents, etc.)
  • Malaria and other febrile illnesses
  • Infections (respiratory, urinary, gastrointestinal, etc.)
  • Dehydration and persistent vomiting or diarrhea
  • Hypertension-related problems
  • Diabetes and other acute medical conditions
  • Abdominal pain and other gastrointestinal complaints
  • Shortness of breath and chest symptoms
  • Wound care and minor procedures
  • Routine medical checkups and health screening
  • Any other urgent non-cancer medical concern
Emergency Maternity Care
Photo placeholder — nurse holding a newborn (images/maternity-emergency.jpg)

Safe care for mothers and newborns

70+
Babies Delivered at COC

Over the past 7 years, COC has assisted with the delivery of 70+ babies who came to us through emergency care.

  • Emergency obstetric assessment and stabilization
  • Management of labor and delivery (when indicated)
  • Care for pregnancy-related complications
  • Newborn care and stabilization
  • Referral to inpatient maternity/obstetric care if needed
  • Support for both mother and baby
